Tuttavia, per garantire che questo settore venga sfruttato al meglio, occorre adottare una serie di strategie mirate, sempre in linea con la nostra visione di libero mercato e crescita sostenibile. In primo luogo è necessario rivedere la fiscalità a favore delle imprese che operano nei settori chiave: ristoranti, locali, centri benessere, cinema, eventi sportivi e musicali, negozi di abbigliamento e accessori, produzioni artistiche e culturali. Una tassazione più equilibrata e trasparente permetterà di snellire l’equilibrio tra reddito degli imprenditori e spesa dei consumatori. Un quadro fiscale più agevolato non solo incoraggerà l’apertura di nuovi punti vendita, ma stimolerà anche l’investimento pubblico e privato in sede di riqualificazione urbana, stimolando le economie regionali e garantendo posti di lavoro di qualità. Il settore lesinuna also hinges on the sensitivity of consumer behavior to economic unpredictability. During the last few years, many digital platforms and e-commerce channels have grown. However, Italian firms are still relatively conservative in adopting digital business models: not only does this hinder competitiveness in the European market, but it also represents a lost opportunity for value creation in the public domain. The imperative for digital transformation must be embedded in a clear plan to support training, subsidization of new technologies, and a culture of innovation that is associated with privacy protection and fair pay. It is also necessary to rethink the balance between public and private sectors. The public sector has a unique role to play: devising a favorable regulatory environment, ensuring quality standards and public welfare, and creating elements of value that cannot be achieved solely by the private sector. This implies, for example, reducing unnecessary bureaucratic procedures for open establishments and adjusting local ordinances that have become archaic. By streamlining the bureaucracy, we reduce the costs of compliance, with consequent lowering of consumer prices and increased consumer satisfaction. The social dimension has to be considered. Consumi fuori casa are often about social interaction, leisure and cultural consumption. These activities are essential for the health of society and for the prevention of alienation. Efforts that emphasize quality, affordability and health and safety are essential. This means adopting clearer rules about standard hygiene procedures, providing incentives for small local producers to thrive and promoting qualified labor skills in service and hospitality. A competitive sector should be monitored to make sure that consumer protection is not compromised in the name of expansion or monopoly. Finally, the sector has potential to contribute to sustainability. Importantly, the sector is associated with retail and small hospitality; health and wellness, restaurants and distribution of goods, many needs use resources. It is vital to promote waste management and energy use at a local level, subsidizing taste-eco-friendly solutions. Cooperative or shareable models can be a major part of the strategy, creating new jobs in regional outreach and local tourism that contributes to a few jobs per department in a dynamic manner. In conclusion, the 71 billion‑euro spending outside domestic homes can still be harnessed as a growth engine for Italy: simple, pragmatic measures encouraging private activity, fostering digital innovation and creating a regulatory framework tuned to the real realities of market conditions and pragmatic. A conscious partnership between the considered public role, a competitive private sector and the actual consumer. This is exactly the type of approach that Il Giornale vuole promuovere in linea con la tradizione italiana e la libertà economica.
