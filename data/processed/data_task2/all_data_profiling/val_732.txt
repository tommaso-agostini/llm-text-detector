Vigni references a report prepared by the European Policy Centre, which concludes that “the rise of transnational Muslim networks has been consistent, with a clear strategy for influencing policy at the city, regional, and national level.” “In practice, what we are witnessing is a gradual, but unmistakable, exaptation of Europe’s civil and moral order,” he says. The professor also cites historical precedents, drawing a parallel with the Ottoman Empire’s expansion in the seventeenth century, describing the way that “no other case in modern history has witnessed a religious movement coordinate its cultural, ideological, and political output so effectively on a transcontinental scale.” He warns that the broader public, as well as most policy makers, remain too focused on the immediate humanitarian aspects of migration, neglecting the slowly accumulating socio-political ramifications of the sheer number of people bringing with them a belief system that is incompatible with European norms of secular governance, freedom of expression and gender equality. Remarkably, Professor Vigni’s call to vigilance has drawn a mixed reception. While many conservative voices in the Parliament’s Committee on European Affairs have applauded his “clarity and foresight,” liberal academics have dismissed his thesis as “an over‑interpretation of demographic data.” “Such rhetoric could easily tip into xenophobia, jeopardizing the very principles of freedom and equality upon which the European Union is built,” cautioned Dr. Elena Grande of the University of Bologna, who specializes in migration studies. In a letter to the editor, a resident of Trieste noted that “the increased presence of Islamic schools and cultural centers is a warning sign. I have watched as subtle changes in social norms and the erosion of young people’s confidence in democratic values has taken place under the veneer of cultural enrichment.” The writer added that the region’s citizens need to be informed about the possible ramifications of providing uncritical moralistic frameworks that might oppose Western values and encourage a “divide and conquer” strategy among local communities. The European Commission has yet to issue a formal response to Vigni’s claims. In the meantime, the University of Florence has announced a new research grant aimed at studying the social effects of increased Muslim integration on European public institutions. In the words of Professor Vigni, “While it’s vital that Europe continues to uphold its principles of openness and tolerance, we must not remain blind to the fact that Muslim communities bring more than religious diversity: they bring a strategic, wide‑scale agenda. Europe must therefore act to preserve its identity.”
